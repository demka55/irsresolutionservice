Questions people ask first

Is this the same as hiring a tax resolution firm?

No. A firm represents you and deals with the IRS for you, which is why it often costs $3,000 or more. The Resolution Sprint helps you do the work yourself: it works out which IRS program fits, prepares your forms and letters, and tells you what to send and by when. You review, sign and file everything yourself.

Are you a law firm, and are you representing me with the IRS?

No. IRS Resolution Service is not a law firm and the Sprint does not represent you before the IRS. It is document preparation and guidance built from Romeo Razi's experience. If you want someone to speak to the IRS for you, the done-for-you option is for that.

What if the AI gets something wrong?

That is why the Romeo Review add-on exists. A CPA reads your finished package before you file it. The Sprint also flags cases that are too risky to do alone, such as an assigned revenue officer or a pending levy, and sends them to a human instead of guessing.

Which IRS problems does it cover?

Common notices such as CP2000, CP14, CP504 and LT11, balances you can't pay in full (payment plans and offers in compromise), currently-not-collectible requests, penalty relief requests, and catching up on unfiled returns. Not covered: criminal matters, active IRS examinations of a business, and trust-fund payroll cases.

Why $200 a month, and how long does it last?

Most IRS cases take one to three months to get to a filed solution. The Sprint is a 3-month plan billed at $200 a month, so the typical case costs about $600 in total. It does not renew on its own after the third month; you can extend if your case needs longer.

Can I cancel?

Yes. You can cancel before your next billing date from your account page.

Can you promise a specific outcome, like a smaller tax bill?

No, and be careful of anyone who does. The IRS decides, based on your income, assets and filing history. What we can do is make sure you apply for the right program, with accurate paperwork, before your deadlines.

What do I need to get started?

Your IRS notice or letter, your last few tax returns if you have them, and about 20 minutes to answer questions on income, expenses and assets. You don't need to be good with technology.

What happens to my documents and information?

They are used only to prepare your case. Don't upload your Social Security number unless a form step asks for it.
