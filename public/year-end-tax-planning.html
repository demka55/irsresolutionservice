📆 Year-End Business Tax Planning

5 tax moves business owners should make before December 31 — and why waiting until January can cost you thousands

Romeo’s take Raw example: why the in-service date can cost $2,880 ↓

The short answer: Year-end tax planning for businesses isn't complicated — but it's time-sensitive. Several of the most valuable moves only work if you execute them before December 31. Prepaying expenses, timing income, purchasing equipment, setting up a retirement plan, and evaluating your entity structure can collectively save thousands. January is too late for most of these.

RAW EXAMPLE · UPDATED NOV. 16, 2026

Why the in-service date can cost $2,880

Meet Hannah, a consultant in the 24% bracket with 45 days until Dec. 31. She needs $12,000 of equipment anyway:

  1. Scenario A: ordered Dec. 1, delivered and set up Dec. 20. With 100% bonus depreciation, she deducts $12,000 in 2026 and saves $2,880.
  2. Scenario B: delivery slips to Jan. 2. The 2026 deduction is $0. She gets it a year later.
  3. Prepaid expenses: paying $3,000 of January costs in December on a cash basis can add another $720 of savings.
  4. Retirement plan: a Solo 401(k) must be adopted by Dec. 31 even though contributions can come later. Total limit for 2026: $72,000.
  5. What she skips: the $5,000 startup credit. It generally needs an eligible non-owner employee, and she has none.
Romeo's take

I tell clients the calendar is the cheapest tax planning tool there is. A good plan in January is too late. A mediocre plan on Dec. 20 still works.

Do not buy something just for the deduction. A $12,000 deduction saves $2,880 and you have still spent $12,000.

  • Nov. 12: the IRS set the 2027 retirement limits: 401(k) deferrals $25,500, age-50 catch-up $8,500, SIMPLE IRA $17,500, IRA unchanged at $7,500 (IR-2026-136). Your 2026 deferral is still capped at $24,500 and has to come out of 2026 pay.
  • Nov. 16: Romeo’s newsletter listed the dates he is watching. We turned it into a year-end deadline calendar, including the new $2,000 threshold for 1099s.
  • After the Nov. 3 midterms: no federal rate has changed, and the 2027 brackets are about 3.2% wider than 2026’s. See the note under Move 2 before you rush income into this year or push it out of it.
  • Still current: 2026 limits are $72,000 total additions, $24,500 employee deferral, $8,000 catch-up for 50+ and $17,000 SIMPLE IRA (Foley summary of Notice 2025-67).

Illustrative example: the people and dollar amounts are made up so the math is easy to follow, and the rules are real. Assumes a 24% bracket and cash-basis accounting. Not tax advice.

Move 1: Prepay business expenses before December 31

If you're on a cash-basis accounting method (which most small businesses are), you can deduct expenses in the year you pay them — not the year they relate to. That means paying January's rent, software subscriptions, or contractor invoices in December creates a deduction on your current-year tax return.

Romeo Razi, CPA — Year-End Tax Newsletter

"Pay January's expenses in December and write them off now. Even on a credit card — the IRS says it counts when you charge it, not on the date you pay the credit card balance. Think rent, software, contractors. But don't be cute — not a 3-year supply of logo hoodies."

The credit card rule is important and often overlooked: if you charge an expense to a business credit card in December, you get the deduction in the current tax year even if you don't pay the credit card until January. The payment is considered made on the date of the charge.

⚠ This only applies to legitimate business expenses you would incur anyway. Accelerating ordinary business expenses is fine. Prepaying for years of supplies you won't use is a gray area — the IRS can challenge prepaid expenses that don't have a clear business reason for the timing.

Move 2: Defer income to January (cash-basis businesses only)

If you haven't issued December invoices yet, consider waiting until January. Delaying when you invoice delays when you receive payment, which delays when you recognize the income for tax purposes.

Critical caveat: this only works if you're on the cash basis of accounting. If you're on the accrual basis, income is recognized when earned — not when cash is received. Holding a December invoice doesn't defer anything on an accrual basis.

Also critical: the payment actually has to arrive in January. A check that arrives December 31 is December income. A payment received January 2 is January income.

Should the midterms change this? Not for 2026. A new Congress is seated January 3, 2027, and proposals to raise the top rate from 37% to 39.6% are expected. None of them is law. On the brackets the IRS published on November 5, income you push into January 2027 is taxed at the same rate or slightly less, because every bracket is about 3.2% wider. State income tax can move faster than federal, so check your state before you defer a large payment. What has been proposed, and what it would take →

Romeo Razi, CPA — Nov. 16, 2026 Newsletter

"For now, sit down, chill, and have some hummus. There will definitely be an unbelievable amount of huffing and puffing regarding taxes going up. But remember Trump still holds the pen, so he will most likely veto anything that tries to dismantle his legacy tax reform over the next two years."

Move 3: Buy equipment before December 31 — and make sure it's in service

With 100% bonus depreciation now permanent for property acquired after January 19, 2025 (see our full guide at Bonus Depreciation 2025), any qualifying business equipment, software, or improvements purchased and placed in service by December 31 can be fully deducted this year.

Romeo Razi, CPA — Year-End Tax Newsletter

"New laptop? Espresso machine? $90K Ford F-250 for your 'construction biz'? Deduct it all this year, as long as you're using it and it's in service by December 31. If it's still in a box or not delivered by January 1st, no dice."

⚠ Vehicles have limits. For 2026, a passenger car is capped at $20,300 of first-year depreciation with bonus, and a heavy SUV over 6,000 lbs is capped at $32,000 of Section 179. Qualifying heavy trucks are treated differently, but you still need real business use and a mileage log. Section 179 itself is limited to $2.56M of purchases for 2026. For 2027 the limit rises to $2.64M and the heavy-SUV cap to $33,100 (Rev. Proc. 2026-35), so a purchase that slips into January is not lost, only a year later.

The in-service requirement is absolute. An asset ordered but not delivered by year-end doesn't qualify. An asset delivered but still boxed and uninstalled in your warehouse on December 31 doesn't qualify. It must be ready and available for use in your business.

Move 4: Start a retirement plan before year-end

Business retirement plans — SEP-IRA, SIMPLE IRA, and Solo 401(k) — offer much larger contribution limits than personal IRAs. Setting one up before year-end creates the opportunity to make deductible contributions that reduce your business income.

SEP
SEP-IRA
Up to 25% of compensation, capped at $72,000 for 2026. Simplest to set up. Can fund up to tax filing deadline including extensions.
Solo
Solo 401(k)
Up to $72,000 for 2026 (your $24,500 employee deferral counts toward it), plus an $8,000 catch-up if you are 50 or older. Must be established by December 31 (though contributions can be made later).
Credit
Startup Credit
Up to $5,000 dollar-for-dollar credit for setting up a new plan, but only if you have at least one eligible non-owner employee. Credit, not just a deduction — reduces your actual tax bill.

Planning for 2027: on November 12 the IRS announced that the 401(k) employee deferral limit rises to $25,500 for 2027, and the age-50 catch-up to $8,500 ($11,750 at ages 60 to 63). SIMPLE plans go to $17,500. The IRA limit does not move; it stays at $7,500. If you run your own payroll, update your deferral election before the first January paycheck.

ⓘ The credit for setting up a new retirement plan is one of the most underused tax benefits for small businesses. If you've never had a retirement plan and you establish one this year, you could get a dollar-for-dollar credit of up to $5,000 on top of the deduction for contributions. This is a credit — meaning it reduces your tax bill by $5,000, not just your taxable income. Check the catch: the credit is for employers with 100 or fewer employees and requires at least one eligible non-highly-compensated employee, so a true solo owner generally does not qualify.

Move 5: Evaluate whether S-Corp status makes sense

If your business had a significantly profitable year and you're operating as an LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship or partnership, an S-Corp election can save substantial amounts in self-employment tax.

The mechanism: as an S-Corp owner-employee, you pay yourself a "reasonable salary" (subject to payroll taxes) and take the remaining profit as a distribution (not subject to self-employment tax). The savings can be thousands or tens of thousands annually for businesses with net income above roughly $50,000–$80,000.

Romeo Razi, CPA — Year-End Tax Newsletter

"Big year? It might be time to upgrade from LLC to S-Corp. S-Corps can save thousands in self-employment tax, but it's not for everyone, no matter what the TikTok gurus tell you. Good news: you can retroactively elect S-Corp status for the current year if you act soon."

⚠ The deadline to elect S-Corp status retroactively for the current tax year is typically March 15 of the following year (or the first day of the tax year for a new entity). However, the window to plan for it and ensure you're in compliance (payroll setup, reasonable compensation analysis) is before December 31. The S-Corp election is also not always the right move — additional compliance costs and complexity need to be weighed against the tax savings.

Three year-end numbers that never get a raise

Most limits on this page rise with inflation every year. These three do not, and each one comes up in December.

$25 per client gift, unchanged since 1962

You can deduct $25 of business gifts per recipient per year, whatever you spend. A $100 holiday basket for a client is a $25 deduction. Small items that cost $4 or less, carry your business name and are handed out widely, such as pens and calendars, do not count toward the $25. A meal you share with a client is not a gift; it falls under the separate 50% rule for business meals.

Romeo Razi, CPA — Nov. 16, 2026 Newsletter

"Business gift deduction: $25 per person. Unchanged since 1962. That bought a nice bottle of scotch back then. Today it almost buys you a half-sandwich."

$3,000 of capital losses against other income, unchanged since 1978

Selling an investment at a loss before December 31 offsets this year’s gains dollar for dollar. If your losses are larger than your gains, only $3,000 of the difference reduces your other income. The rest carries forward to later years. Do not buy the same investment back within 30 days before or after the sale, or the wash-sale rule disallows the loss.

$25,000 of rental losses, unchanged since 1986

If you actively participate in a rental property, up to $25,000 of its loss can offset your wages and business income. The allowance shrinks by 50 cents for every dollar of modified adjusted gross income over $100,000 and is gone at $150,000. If you are close to that range, deferring income into January (Move 2) can preserve part of it for 2026.

Romeo Razi, CPA — Nov. 16, 2026 Newsletter

"Remember, the IRS adjusts the numbers which Congress tells them. Those make headlines and the rest is left in the freezer. That's not an accident, it's on purpose."

Frequently asked questions about year-end business tax planning

I'm on the accrual basis — can I still defer income by holding invoices?
Generally no. Accrual-basis taxpayers recognize income when it's earned and all events have occurred to fix the right to receive it — not when cash is received. Holding an invoice until January doesn't delay income recognition if the services were already performed. There are some specific timing rules for accrual-basis taxpayers, but they're more technical and limited.
How much does an S-Corp actually save in self-employment taxes?
Self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on net self-employment income. In an S-Corp, only your salary is subject to payroll taxes — not your distributions. If you're paying $180,000 out of an LLC, all $180,000 is subject to self-employment tax (~$25,000+ in SE tax). As an S-Corp with a $100,000 salary and $80,000 distribution, only the salary is taxed (~$15,000). Rough savings: $10,000+. The specific math depends on your situation.
Can I set up a Solo 401(k) right now if I'm a sole proprietor?
Yes — but the plan document must be signed and adopted by December 31 of the tax year you want to make contributions for. This is a common area where people miss the deadline: they intend to set up the plan but don't get the paperwork done by year-end. The contributions themselves can typically be made up to the filing deadline including extensions. But the establishment deadline is December 31 — don't miss it.
What year-end tax deadlines should a business owner have on the calendar?
December 15, 2026 for a calendar-year C corporation’s fourth-quarter estimate. December 31 for equipment in service, prepaid expenses, adopting a Solo 401(k), 2026 401(k) employee deferrals and required minimum distributions at 73 or older. January 15, 2027 for an individual’s fourth-quarter estimate. February 1, 2027 for Forms 1099-NEC, because January 31 falls on a Sunday.
What is the 1099 reporting threshold for payments made in 2026?
$2,000, up from $600. It applies to payments made during 2026 and reported on Forms 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC due by February 1, 2027, and it rises to $2,100 for 2027 payments. Income below the threshold is still taxable to the person who received it, so the change reduces paperwork, not tax.
Should I pull income into 2026 because the new Congress might raise taxes?
For most owners, no. No rate change is law. Raising a rate takes a bill signed by the president or a two-thirds override in both chambers, and the official 2027 brackets are about 3.2% wider than 2026’s, so deferred income is taxed the same or slightly less. Romeo Razi, CPA, says in his November 16, 2026 newsletter that he does not expect a federal tax increase to become law before 2029. Check your state separately, since state legislatures can move faster.
