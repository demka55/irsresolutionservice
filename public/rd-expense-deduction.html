⚙ R&D Expense Deduction — Section 174A
By Romeo Razi, CPA — Former IRS Tax Examiner ·Updated October 26, 2026 ·★★★★★ 5.0 on Google (8 client reviews)

R&D expenses are deductible again. The window to amend 2022–2024 returns has closed — here's what you can still recover.

The short answer: The One Big Beautiful Act added Section 174A, which lets businesses deduct domestic R&D costs immediately for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024. Eligible small businesses (average gross receipts of $31 million or less) could also amend their 2022–2024 returns to deduct costs they'd been forced to amortize — but that election closed on July 6, 2026. Every business can still deduct its remaining unamortized 2022–2024 domestic R&D balance, either all on the 2025 return or split across 2025 and 2026, and many can still claim R&D tax credits for open years.

⏱ Retroactive amendment election closed July 6, 2026 — the 2025/2026 catch-up deduction is still available
▶

What changed and why it was a problem

Before 2022, companies could deduct research and development expenses in the year they were incurred — just like any other business expense. If you paid $500,000 in programmer salaries and hardware costs for a software project, you deducted $500,000.

A provision in the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act changed this starting in 2022: R&D expenses had to be amortized over 5 years (15 years for foreign R&D). Because of the mid-year convention, the same $500,000 produced only a $50,000 deduction in year one. The cash was out the door, but the deduction was spread forward.

Romeo Razi, CPA — Explaining the One Big Beautiful Act

"A bunch of startup people were complaining that they had no money and they kept paying money to the government in taxes. Because all the cash would go out for the programmers and the hardware, but then they had to amortize R&D over 5 years — so they'd end up paying tax on what was basically a paper profit. This was a big deal for both Republicans and Democrats. So they fixed it. And not only that — they made it so if you did amortize it over the last few years, you can go back and amend your tax return and get your tax dollars back."

When Romeo recorded that explanation, the amendment window was open. Under the IRS's implementing guidance (Rev. Proc. 2025-28), it applied only to small businesses that met the $31 million gross-receipts test, and the election had to be made by July 6, 2026 — or earlier, if a given year's refund deadline ran out first. That date has now passed.

Section 174 vs. Section 174A at a glance

Old §174 (tax years 2022–2024) New §174A (tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2024)
Domestic R&DCapitalize and amortize over 5 yearsDeduct immediately, or elect to amortize
Foreign R&DAmortize over 15 yearsStill amortized over 15 years
Software developmentTreated as R&D, amortizedTreated as R&D, deductible
Unamortized 2022–2024 balance—Deduct all in 2025, or split over 2025 and 2026

What you can still do now

If you missed July 6 — or your business was too large to qualify — the money from 2022–2024 isn't gone. It comes back through your 2025 and 2026 returns instead of amended ones.

2025–26
Catch-up deduction
Any business can deduct its remaining unamortized 2022–2024 domestic R&D balance in full on its 2025 return, or ratably over 2025 and 2026. If your 2025 return is filed, have your preparer confirm which option was used — with the two-year split, part of the deduction belongs on your 2026 return.
2025+
Current-year expensing
Domestic R&D paid or incurred in 2025 and later is deductible in the year you pay it. Check that your 2025 return actually took the deduction — a return prepared on habit may still have amortized it.
§41
R&D tax credit
Credit claims can still go on amended returns for years whose refund window is open — generally 3 years from filing or 2 years from payment. For many calendar-year businesses, 2023 and 2024 are still open.

⚠ The related reduced-credit election (§280C(c)(2)) also closed July 6, 2026, so adding a research credit on an amended return now may require reducing the expense deduction. Model both before you file anything for 2022–2024.

If your business also filed or paid late anywhere in the 2020–2023 window, it's worth checking one more thing while your preparer is in those years: a separate court decision may mean you overpaid IRS penalties for that period. See our guide on Kwong COVID penalty refunds.

New since September 2026: fixing a 2025 return that got R&D wrong

The October 15, 2026 extended deadline for calendar-year C corporations and individuals has passed, which means most 2025 returns are now filed. If yours still amortized domestic R&D under the old rules, or handled the 2022–2024 balance incorrectly, there's a newer fix.

  • Rev. Proc. 2026-32 (September 2026) expands the automatic accounting-method-change procedures for research costs. Businesses that didn't follow the right method can generally switch to expensing domestic R&D from 2025 forward without IRS consent, using Form 3115 (many of these changes use designated change number 273), and for some 2025 changes a simpler statement in place of a full Form 3115.
  • The usual five-year waiting rule is waived for research method changes in tax years beginning before January 1, 2028, so a business that changed methods recently can still use the automatic route.
  • Transition date: November 15, 2026. Taxpayers that file the duplicate Form 3115 on or before that date can choose between the old and the updated procedures.
  • Foreign research stays under the 15-year amortization rule, and the automatic change for foreign R&E now extends to 2026 and later years.

ⓘ Method changes interact with the one-year vs. two-year catch-up choice for the 2022–2024 balance, because the §481(a) adjustment is calculated based on which recovery option you elected. This is preparer territory — but it's worth asking your preparer directly whether your 2025 return used §174A.

R&D tax credit vs. R&D deduction: can you claim both?

Yes — most businesses with qualifying research use both, but they interact.

§174A deduction §41 research credit
What it doesReduces taxable incomeReduces tax dollar-for-dollar
What countsBroad: research and experimental costs, including software developmentNarrower: qualified research expenses that pass the four-part test
Where it's claimedYour business returnForm 6765, carried to Form 3800
If you can't use it this yearIncreases your net operating lossGenerally carries back 1 year and forward 20 years; qualified startups can apply up to $500,000 a year against payroll taxes instead

The interaction: you generally must reduce the §174A deduction by the amount of the credit, or elect a reduced credit instead. Run both ways before filing. Starting with tax years beginning after 2025, most taxpayers must also complete the new Section G of Form 6765, which asks for project-level detail — so documentation habits matter now, not at filing time.

State returns don't always follow. States conform to the federal code on their own schedules. California, for example, never adopted the 2022 federal amortization rule, so California returns generally kept deducting R&D. Check your state before assuming the federal answer carries over.

What qualifies as R&D for this deduction

The tax definition of research and experimental (R&E) expenses is broader than most people expect. It covers costs incurred in connection with your trade or business that represent research or experimentation in the experimental or laboratory sense. Common qualifying costs:

  • Software development costs (salaries, contractors, cloud services used in development)
  • Engineering and product development labor
  • Testing and quality assurance tied to development
  • Prototype development
  • Clinical trials and drug development
  • Third-party research performed under contract

Costs that don't qualify include routine quality control, market research, advertising, and management studies.

Frequently asked questions about R&D deductions

Can you claim both the R&D tax credit and the R&D deduction?
Yes. Most businesses with qualifying research claim the Section 174A deduction and the Section 41 credit in the same year. You generally must reduce the deduction by the credit amount or elect a reduced credit, so model both before filing.
My 2025 return still amortized R&D. How do I fix it?
Rev. Proc. 2026-32, issued in September 2026, expanded automatic accounting method changes for research costs, so many businesses can switch to expensing domestic R&D without IRS consent using Form 3115. The usual five-year eligibility limit is waived for tax years beginning before January 1, 2028. Have your preparer review which recovery option you chose for the 2022-2024 balance, since it affects the adjustment.
Does California follow Section 174A?
California never adopted the 2022 federal requirement to amortize research costs, so California returns generally continued deducting R&D. Each state sets its own conformity, so check your state's rules separately from the federal return.
Can I still amend my 2022, 2023 or 2024 return to deduct R&D expenses?
Generally no. The retroactive election under Rev. Proc. 2025-28 was available only to small businesses meeting the $31 million gross-receipts test, and it had to be made by July 6, 2026 (or earlier if a year's refund deadline expired first). Businesses that didn't make it can still deduct their remaining unamortized 2022-2024 domestic R&D balance, either all on the 2025 return or split across 2025 and 2026.
What is the difference between Section 174 and Section 174A?
Section 174, as changed by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, required businesses to capitalize research costs and amortize them over 5 years (domestic) or 15 years (foreign) for tax years 2022 through 2024. Section 174A, added by the One Big Beautiful Act, lets businesses deduct domestic research costs immediately for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024, or elect to amortize them. Foreign research costs still must be amortized over 15 years.
What happens to R&D costs I was already amortizing from 2022-2024?
Any business with a remaining unamortized balance of domestic research costs from 2022-2024 can deduct it in full in the first tax year beginning after December 31, 2024 (2025 for calendar-year businesses), or ratably over that year and the next. If you chose the two-year option, part of the deduction belongs on your 2026 return.
Can I still claim the R&D tax credit for prior years?
Often yes. Research credit claims under Section 41 can be made on amended returns for years whose refund window is still open, generally three years from filing or two years from payment. For many calendar-year businesses 2023 and 2024 are still open. Because the related reduced-credit election also closed July 6, 2026, adding a credit now may require reducing the deduction, so model both before filing.
I'm a startup that had losses anyway — does the R&D deduction change matter for me?
The immediate deduction may not change your cash tax if you already had losses exceeding your income. But a larger deduction can increase your net operating loss, which carries forward to offset future income. For startups expecting to become profitable, that can be significant.
